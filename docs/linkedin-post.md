When an account manager leaves an agency, the client relationship shouldn't have to start over.

The problem is rarely lost files. The shared drive still holds every contract, presentation deck, and recorded meeting transcript.

The problem is that the institutional context lived inside the human's head.

An account manager spends two years learning what a client hates, what they approved, what ideas failed six months ago, and which executive actually signs off on design adjustments. When that person rolls off the account, the new account manager repeats old mistakes, re-proposes rejected concepts, and asks questions the client answered months ago.

We built Viora to solve this institutional amnesia.

Viora turns client meeting conversations into durable, client-isolated institutional memory.

The account manager does not need to manually highlight notes or tag categories. Raw conversation transcripts are ingested directly. Powered by Hindsight as the memory infrastructure, the system extracts durable business facts: client preferences, explicit rejections, technical constraints, and stakeholder approval thresholds.

When someone new steps into the account, they do not have to guess. They ask Viora direct questions:

"What database technologies did the client explicitly reject?"
"Who holds approval authority for creative scope adjustments?"
"Did the portal launch timeline change across meetings?"

Viora recalls the relevant facts from the client's isolated memory bank, links them to verbatim transcript quotes and chronological dates, and delivers a grounded answer.

For example, when an account manager took over Meridian Logistics, Viora immediately surfaced that the client's VP of Technology had strictly rejected MongoDB in January, mandated PostgreSQL on AWS Aurora, and later approved TimescaleDB as an extension for telemetry in March. It also surfaced that the Product Director had been delegated $5,000 approval authority for creative assets.

The new account manager avoided proposing a rejected database, routed creative approvals to the right executive, and continued the partnership without missing a beat.

Institutional client memory belongs to the agency, not just the individual.
